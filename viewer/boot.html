    What this is

    • Built on a stand-in foot (a CC0 research scan) until Jon's own scans exist.
    • Lines placed from the scan: the ankle bones set the cuff and the ankle pockets; the ridge from the toes up the shin sets the throat, tongue and eyelets.
    • Tight from heel to toe, open at the top. The cuff stops just above the outer ankle bone at the sides (40 mm), rising up the shin at the front for the lacing and into the tendon guard at the back. Above the ankle bones the liner and shell flare out together: 13 mm of room at the sides (about 19° of ankle tip each way), 7 mm in front, a slight press at the back to pitch the leg forward. Cut through the ankle to see it.
    • Two throats. Open, for stiff boots like this one: over the forefoot and instep the opening is half the way from the top of the foot to its widest line, measured along the surface the same on both sides, so it sits square on the top of the foot; the vamp is short, the opening starting just behind the toe cap. V, for shoes and more flexible boots: a narrow throat from the toe cap widening up the shin. Either way the eyelets run in mirrored pairs, evenly spaced, and splay into a V up the shin for forward flex; the heel-lock pair at the ankle crease is heavy duty.
    • The tongue is 3D-printed TPU, like True's: a solid plate (the outer skin the laces bear on, a rim, an inner skin) round a gyroid lattice core that breathes and squashes. It is thin under the shell's edges and swells between them, its edges rounded, its top flaring forward over the shin. Explode the boot to see the lattice.
    • Two editions on the same boot: all black, and an alt in white fibreglass weave, royal blue, red accents and a speckled cement-grey heel panel, inspired by a sneaker colourway. Or make your own under Custom: every part's colour and finish.
    • The look: black carbon shell against black thermoplastic (toe cap, mount pads, tendon guard), a black printed tongue, white hockey laces. Leaned forward 6°, a padded collar over the shell's edge, a smooth outer shell (its inside still follows the foot).
    • The sole is just two flat mount pads, heel and forefoot, where the holder or roller chassis attaches; between them the shell's own rounded underside.
    • The roller boot is vented (toe cap, lower quarters, liner); the ice boot is sealed, since a cold rink and melting ice make vents a liability.
    • Under the roller boot: the round-2 rocker chassis (ribbed upright walls, 4 x 72 mm wheels, the upper rocking on a 7.5 ft arc). Under the ice boot: a first look at the holder, not yet designed: two windowed towers on a beam, our own steel runner rocking against our own insert in its channel, on a pivot pin. Its sizes are estimates. Explode to drop them away from the boot.
    • Cuff, tongue and tendon guard heights are estimates until measured on the TF9.
    • Weighed with the shell, toe cap and mount pads in 3D-printed nylon (PA 12); a carbon shell would be laid up thinner. Foam, lace and TPU densities are assumed.
